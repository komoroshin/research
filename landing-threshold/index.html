Elimination is the easy half

Cutting foods out is a list. Bringing them back is a test — and it has rules: a fixed dose ladder over three days, a washout between challenges, a pass criterion, a decision about what to do next.

Those rules exist. They're published. They're just not in your app — they're in a blog post you're supposed to keep open in another tab while you remember which day you're on.

So most people stay in elimination. Months longer than the protocol calls for, on a diet that was never meant to be permanent.

The protocol, run for you

  1. 1

    Baseline — 7 to 10 days

    Change nothing. Log meals by photo or voice, answer a 20-second symptom check each evening. This is the reference point everything else is measured against.

  2. 2

    Elimination — 2 to 6 weeks

    If you're already here, we start from where you are. Daily guidance: what's out, what to eat instead, what's safe when eating out.

  3. 3

    Reintroduction — 4 to 6 weeks

    One group at a time. Each morning Threshold tells you the day, the dose and what to eat. Each evening it collects the result. It runs the washout, applies the pass criterion and schedules the next challenge. You don't track the protocol — it tracks itself.

  4. 4

    Your map

    Group by group: tolerated, not tolerated, or tolerated up to a specific amount. Plus a one-page summary for your gastroenterologist.

Not a list of what you can't eat. A map of what you can.

Most people finish elimination with a shorter diet and no more knowledge than when they started. The point of reintroduction is the opposite: you find out which groups are actually a problem for you, and how much of each you tolerate.

Half a cup of milk, fine. A full glass, not. Wheat in a small portion, fine. That's the difference between avoiding a food group forever and eating in a restaurant without guessing.

How this is different

  • A food database tells you what's in a food. Threshold tells you what to do today, and what your last three challenges mean for the next one.
  • A symptom diary stores what you type. Threshold reads it: it finds which groups your symptoms follow, with what delay, across how many occurrences.
  • A six-week course is the same for everyone. Threshold branches. Two people with different answers take different paths through the protocol.

What Threshold is not

Threshold does not diagnose, and it does not treat. It runs a published dietary protocol and shows you what your own data says.

Some symptoms need a doctor before any diet: blood in stool, unintended weight loss, symptoms that wake you at night, or new symptoms after age 50. If you report any of these, Threshold stops and tells you to see a clinician. It will not work around them.

If you're pregnant, managing an eating disorder, or already under a dietitian's care, talk to them before starting.

Questions

Can't I just do this myself?
The protocol is published, so yes, in principle. In practice the reintroduction phase is where people stop: it requires holding a schedule, doses, washouts and a pass criterion across six weeks while symptoms come and go. The ACG guideline itself notes the diet is complex enough to warrant a trained dietitian. Threshold is the part that keeps the schedule.
Is this a dietitian?
No. Threshold runs the protocol and organises your data. It does not replace clinical care, and there is no clinician reviewing your case in this version.
What if the diet doesn't help me?
Then the protocol says so. If your symptoms don't respond during elimination, Threshold tells you that this route isn't working for you rather than keeping you on a restricted diet.
Do I need to buy anything else?
No. No device, no lab test, no supplements. Your phone is the only thing the protocol needs.
What happens to my data?
It stays yours. Export or delete at any time.
